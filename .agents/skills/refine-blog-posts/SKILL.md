# Refine Blog Posts

## Editorial goal

Make the prose more compact without flattening it. Let logical continuity, shared ideas, and dramatic progression organize paragraphs. Treat whitespace and typographic emphasis as scarce expressive tools whose value depends on restraint.

## Workflow

1. Read every relevant post and language variant in full before editing.
2. Read repository instructions and inspect the working tree. Preserve unrelated user changes and all required file conventions.
3. Map the existing narrative arc: opening promise, personal stakes, conflict, reversals, core thesis, consequence, and closing image or conclusion.
4. Identify repetitions, overextended lists, and consecutive short paragraphs that express one thought. Decide which beats genuinely need isolation before rewriting.
5. Rewrite the posts directly. Preserve facts, intent, voice, point of view, recurring motifs, and emotional honesty. Do not invent supporting details or neutralize strong opinions.
6. Re-read each result as continuous prose, then review only the isolated and emphasized sentences. Ensure those sentences form the structural and emotional spine of the piece.
7. Compare language variants for equivalent meaning, narrative progression, emphasis, and ending. Keep each language idiomatic rather than forcing identical syntax or paragraph length.
8. Review the diff and run the repository's required validation commands.

## Paragraph structure

- Merge adjacent one-sentence paragraphs when they develop the same idea.
- Give each ordinary paragraph one coherent function and enough room to develop it.
- Compress mechanical repetition and list-like sequences into flowing prose when the rhythm does not depend on the list.
- Retain short fragments when they create a deliberate pause, escalation, reversal, or payoff.
- Isolate only genuine turns: an aha moment, a thesis, a decisive contrast, a revelation, or a closing line that completes the arc.
- Avoid hard targets for word count, paragraph count, or sentence length. Tighten only as far as the material supports.

## Emphasis

- Use **bold** for decisive claims, core theses, and the strongest turns.
- Use *italics* for quieter realizations, tonal pivots, irony, or reflective counterpoints.
- Emphasize only the essential sentence or phrase, not a whole ordinary paragraph.
- Allow a short thesis paragraph to be fully emphasized only when every sentence in it earns the emphasis.
- Do not emphasize every memorable line. Too many isolated or styled sentences destroy the hierarchy.
- Keep blank space before and after the most important isolated beats.

## Compression rules

- Cut duplicated explanation before cutting concrete images, specific experiences, or recurring motifs.
- Preserve the reasoning that connects claims; do not turn an argument into disconnected slogans.
- Combine examples that prove the same point, but retain the strongest or most personal example.
- Replace repeated sentence openings only when repetition is not serving rhythm or escalation.
- Prefer a clean transition over an extra paragraph that restates the previous conclusion.
- Keep the ending earned. Preserve setup details that the closing image or thesis calls back to.

## Multilingual variants

- Edit every requested language version separately.
- Preserve the same essential claims and dramatic architecture across variants.
- Choose natural wording, rhythm, idioms, punctuation, and emphasis for each language.
- Do not introduce a claim, image, or level of certainty in one language that is absent from the other.
- Perform a final side-by-side check of titles, section headings, key examples, thesis sentences, and conclusions.

## Quality check

Confirm that:

- the post still sounds like its author;
- no required idea or factual qualifier disappeared during compression;
- most paragraph breaks reflect a change in thought, function, or dramatic beat;
- isolated lines remain rare enough to feel significant;
- bold and italic styling establish hierarchy instead of decoration;
- the opening creates a promise that the conclusion resolves;
- multilingual versions remain equivalent but idiomatic;
- only intended files changed and all required checks pass.
